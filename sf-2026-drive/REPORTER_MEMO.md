# Where San Francisco voted how: precinct findings for reporters

> **Status.** These numbers are from the Department of Elections' preliminary Report 11, released November 10, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. Pacific. Counting continues until certification, due December 3, and every number here can still move. Say a measure is "passing" or "leading," not that it passed. Cite the report number with any figure you use.

> **Read this first: these are neighborhood patterns, not how any group voted.** Everything below compares precinct results with what the census says about the people who live in each precinct. That can tell you a candidate ran stronger in precincts with more Asian residents. It cannot tell you Asian voters backed that candidate. The voters in a precinct are not the same as its residents, and a precinct-level pattern can hold even if the group in question voted the other way. Don't write that a group voted a certain way on the strength of this memo, and don't let a headline or caption say it either.
>
> | Write this | Not this |
> | --- | --- |
> | Hilton ran strongest in precincts with more Asian and foreign-born residents. | Asian voters backed Hilton. / Immigrants swung to Hilton. |
> | Measure B did best in precincts with more Hispanic residents and more renters. | Latino voters and renters passed Measure B. |
> | Ballots counted later were more favorable to Measure B than those counted first. | Young voters / progressives carried Measure B late. |

## Top findings

1. **The late count changed the local measures.** Ballots counted after November 4 were more yes on Measures B and I and more no on Measures D and G, in the large majority of precincts. That moved Measure B from trailing to leading and cut Measure I's deficit, but not by enough.
2. **Measure G lost even on the ocean side.** The Great Highway measure is at 34.3 percent yes. The two districts along the ocean were its best, and both were still under half: District 1 at 49.6 percent and District 4 at 49.4 percent.
3. **CA-11 split the west side from the north.** Scott Wiener leads Connie Chan 53.3 percent to 46.7 percent. Chan leads in the Sunset, the Outer Richmond, the Mission, Bayview, and Chinatown. Wiener leads in the Inner Richmond, the Castro, Noe Valley, and most of the north side, from the Marina to Pacific Heights.
4. **Hilton ran strongest in the west and south.** Steve Hilton has 15.3 percent of the two-candidate vote in the city, from 23.6 percent in District 4 down to 7.5 percent in District 8.
5. **Measures B and I drew on the same parts of the city.** Both were strongest in the center and the east, and both did best in District 9. Measure I ran behind B in every supervisor district, by 2.4 to 6.2 points, and is trailing.
6. **Measure D split north and south, not east and west.** It is 67.5 percent yes in District 2 and under half in four districts: 9, 11, 4, and 5.

## The late count

Report 5, released November 4, was the last report with No ahead on Measure B. Since then the city has counted 145,182 more ballots, on top of the 206,320 counted by November 4.

| Measure (official ballot title) | Yes share, votes counted by Nov. 4 | Yes share, votes counted since | Now |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| B, Establishing A Municipal Finance Corporation And A Public Bank | 49.96% | 56.6% | 52.7% yes, leading by 17,163 |
| I, Changes To Real Property Transfer Tax | 45.3% | 52.8% | 48.3% yes, trailing by 10,686 |
| D, Changes To Ballot Measure Process | 57.5% | 49.3% | 54.2% yes, leading |
| G, Allowing Private Vehicles On The Great Highway In Sunset Dunes Park | 36.4% | 31.3% | 34.3% yes, trailing |

- **It happened inside precincts.** The later votes were more yes on Measure B in 439 of 506 precincts compared, and less yes on Measure D in 474 of 506. So this is not a story about which neighborhoods were counted last. Ballots counted later in the same precinct leaned differently from the ones counted first.
- **It got stronger toward the end.** The votes added on November 5 through 7 were 54.1 to 56.2 percent yes on Measure B. Those added on November 8 through 10 were 67.0 to 69.5 percent yes.
- **Measure B took the lead on the November 5 report** and has led on every report since.
- **Measure I has trailed on every report.** No's lead shrank from 18,064 votes on November 4 to 10,686.
- **Measure D has led on every report,** but its yes share fell from 59.80 percent on election night to 54.19 percent.

**Can Measure I still pass?** The Department has not said how many ballots are left. If the rest break like the last two reports did, Measure I needs about 37,800 more ballots to pull even. The last two reports added 5,094 and 3,564. Ask the Department how many remain before you write that it can't catch up.

**What we can't tell you:** who the late voters are. A common explanation is that some groups of voters return mail ballots later. If you use it, it needs a source, such as the Department or a political scientist, not this data.

## Measure G, the Great Highway

- Yes 112,765, No 215,803, 34.3 percent yes.
- It has the widest precinct range of the six contests here, and it is the one local measure with a sharp east-west split.
- **Best districts:** District 1 at 49.6 percent and District 4 at 49.4 percent, then District 11 at 45.0 percent.
- **Worst districts:** District 8 at 21.3 percent, District 5 at 22.6 percent, and District 6 at 25.2 percent.
- **Census pattern:** yes ran higher in precincts with more Asian residents and lower in precincts with more renters.

## CA-11

- Wiener 160,400 (53.3 percent), Chan 140,395 (46.7 percent). Both are Democrats.
- **Where Chan led:** the Sunset, the Outer Richmond, the Mission, Bayview, and Chinatown.
- **Where Wiener led:** the Inner Richmond, the Castro, Noe Valley, and most of the north side, from the Marina to Pacific Heights.
- **By supervisor district, CA-11 precincts only:** Chan has 59.0 percent in District 4, 53.4 percent in District 9, and 51.2 percent in District 1. She has 37.4 percent in District 8 and 37.6 percent in District 2.
- **Census pattern:** Chan ran lower in precincts with more non-Hispanic white residents and with more college graduates.
- **Caution:** 61 of the city's 514 precincts, along the southern edge, are in CA-15. Supervisor District 11 is mostly in CA-15 and has only 1,784 votes in this race.

## Governor inside San Francisco

- Xavier Becerra 287,632, Steve Hilton 51,897. Hilton has 15.3 percent of the two-candidate vote.
- **Strongest districts:** District 4 at 23.6 percent and District 11 at 20.7 percent.
- **Weakest districts:** District 8 at 7.5 percent, then District 9 at 9.8 percent and District 5 at 10.0 percent.
- **Census pattern:** Hilton ran stronger in precincts with more Asian residents and more foreign-born residents.
- This section is where the warning at the top matters most. Hilton's precinct shares run from 7.0 percent to 15.4 percent to 25.4 percent (10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles), so in nine of ten precincts Becerra had about three-quarters of the vote or more.

## Measures B and I

- **Measure B:** yes 169,415, no 152,252, 52.7 percent yes.
- **Measure I:** yes 155,384, no 166,070, 48.3 percent yes.
- Both were strongest in District 9 (64.3 percent yes on B, 59.2 percent on I) and District 5 (62.3 percent and 57.9 percent).
- Both were weakest in District 2 (41.3 percent and 37.4 percent).
- **Census pattern:** both ran higher in precincts with more Hispanic residents and more renters, and lower where more households make $150,000 or more.

## Measure D

- Yes 178,653, no 151,011, 54.2 percent yes.
- **The north voted yes heavily.** District 2 is at 67.5 percent, and Districts 6, 8, and 7 are at 58.5, 58.3, and 57.4 percent.
- **Under half:** District 9 (the Mission, Bernal Heights, and Portola) at 44.4 percent, District 11 at 47.8 percent, District 4 at 48.8 percent, and District 5 at 49.3 percent.
- **Census pattern:** yes ran higher in precincts with more non-Hispanic white residents and more college graduates, and lower in precincts with more Hispanic residents.

## Turnout

- Turnout is 65.63 percent, 351,502 ballots. It is lower in the southeast.
- **On hold:** precinct 9723 in Lakeshore, which includes San Francisco State, reports 840 ballots against 772 registered voters. The Department has not explained it yet. Don't write about turnout around Lake Merced or SF State until it does.

## What this memo doesn't cover

The supervisor races, the Board of Education, the Community College Board, the state propositions, and the other statewide races. The other seven local measures are all passing and show no sharp geographic split; see `FINDINGS.md`.

## How this was done

- Every number here comes from `FINDINGS.md`, and the notebooks in the Drive folder recompute each one from the Department's files.
- Shares are the named side's share of the two-candidate vote, or of yes plus no.
- District figures count every vote in the district.
- Census comparisons use 2020 census and 2020–2024 American Community Survey figures. They count only precincts with at least 50 ballots and few residents in group housing such as dorms.
